# Common failure patterns

Most low scores follow a handful of recognizable profiles. Each one has a structural cause and a predictable wrong response.

## The well-written graveyard

**Profile:** high Clarity, low Trust.

The documentation reads beautifully. It was written two years ago during a docs initiative, and nobody has owned it since. People stopped believing it after the first few bad experiences.

**Wrong response:** another rewrite.

**Right response:** assign owners, add review dates, and archive what nobody will maintain.

## The search tool rescue

**Profile:** high Speed, low Clarity and Trust.

The organization bought enterprise search or an AI assistant to fix findability. Now people find stale, unclear content faster than before.

**Wrong response:** tune the search tool.

**Right response:** fix the content and ownership underneath it. Search quality is capped by source quality.

## The hero archive

**Profile:** high Trust, low Speed and Reusability.

A few senior people know everything, and everyone trusts them. Knowledge flows through their calendars. It works until one of them goes on leave, burns out, or resigns.

**Wrong response:** ask the heroes to write more.

**Right response:** pair capture with real work. Record decisions as they happen, and give the heroes credit for feeding the system. Systems outperform heroics.

## The deck factory

**Profile:** decent Speed and Clarity, very low Reusability.

Teams produce strong, polished deliverables. Every one is built from scratch and stored where nobody else will find it.

**Wrong response:** a shared drive folder called "Final decks."

**Right response:** break recurring content into owned components with one source for core facts.

## The AI content flood

**Profile:** rising volume, falling Trust.

A team adopted generative AI and publishing volume went up sharply. Readers now can't tell which pages a person reviewed and which ones nobody checked.

**Wrong response:** generate more, faster.

**Right response:** require ownership and review for anything published, and measure flow instead of output. See [Thoughtful Velocity and AI](../ai.md).
