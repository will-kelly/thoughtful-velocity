# Reading your score

The index number matters less than two other things: which factor is lowest, and how far it sits below the others.

## Find the constraint first

Your lowest factor is your constraint. Fix it before anything else. Improving any other factor produces a fraction of the return, because the weak one caps the result.

If two factors tie for lowest, start with the one that's cheaper to move. Trust is usually cheapest, because it needs governance decisions more than new content.

## Watch the spread

A flat profile like 2, 2, 2, 2 means the organization hasn't invested in knowledge flow anywhere. The fix is a program, not a project.

A spiky profile like 4, 3, 1, 3 means the organization has invested heavily in some factors and ignored one. The fix is narrow and usually fast, and it puts the existing investment to work.

Spiky profiles are more common than flat ones, and they're better news.

## Rough bands

| Index | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| Under 10 | At least one factor has effectively collapsed. People route around your knowledge systems. |
| 10 to 30 | Knowledge helps in pockets. Most of its value leaks out through one weak factor. |
| 30 to 60 | Knowledge flow works for most common cases. The remaining constraint shows up in edge cases and new hires. |
| Over 60 | Rare. Knowledge is a working part of how the organization operates. Protect it through reorgs and tool changes. |

## Don't report the number upward

The index is for finding the constraint, not for a board slide. Once you put it on a scorecard, teams will optimize the number instead of the flow.

Report the constraint, the evidence behind it, and what fixing it is worth in time and risk. That's the conversation leadership needs to have.
