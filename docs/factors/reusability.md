# Reusability

## Definition

Reusability is whether knowledge can be used again in another context without someone rebuilding it. Reusable knowledge is structured, modular, and stored somewhere other people and systems can reach.

Reusability is where knowledge compounds. Every reuse is work the organization didn't have to pay for twice.

## What zero looks like

- The same security questionnaire answer gets rewritten for every deal.
- Strong analysis lives in a slide deck from last year that nobody can find.
- Every team maintains its own version of the product overview.
- Knowledge leaves the building when a senior person does.
- AI pilots stall because the content can't be chunked, tagged, or retrieved cleanly.

## Structural causes

Reusability collapses when knowledge gets locked inside one-off deliverables. Decks, emails, meeting recordings, and long-form documents hold real answers, but in a shape nothing else can use.

The incentive problem makes it worse. People get credit for delivering the deck, not for making its contents reusable. So every project produces assets and almost none of them feed a system.

## Diagnostic questions

1. Pick a recurring deliverable such as an RFP response, a customer briefing, or an architecture review. How much of it gets written from scratch each time?
2. Is your content structured with consistent types, metadata, and modular sections, or is it free-form pages?
3. Could an AI retrieval system use your top documents today without cleanup?
4. When a senior expert leaves, what share of what they knew is captured somewhere others can use?
5. Do you have a single source for core facts like product descriptions, policies, and approved claims, or are they copied into every document?

## What moves it

- Break recurring knowledge into reusable components with clear owners.
- Keep core facts in one place and reference them instead of copying them.
- Add structure and metadata so people and machines can retrieve the right piece.
- Reward contributions to the system as well as delivery of the asset.
- Capture expert knowledge before it's needed, not during the exit interview.

Reusability is the factor that matters most for AI. Retrieval, agents, and copilots all depend on knowledge that's already structured to be reused.
