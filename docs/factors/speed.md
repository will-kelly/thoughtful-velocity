# Speed

## Definition

Speed is the time between a person having a question and that person having an answer they can use. It covers finding the knowledge, getting access to it, and getting it in a form that fits the moment.

Speed isn't how fast you publish. A team can publish daily and still have terrible speed, because publishing and retrieval are different problems.

## What zero looks like

- People ask in Slack before they search, because asking is faster.
- The same question gets answered in multiple channels every week.
- Search returns 200 results and the right one is on page three.
- New hires measure their ramp time in months and describe it as "learning who to ask."
- Access requests stand between people and documents they're supposed to use.

## Structural causes

Speed usually collapses because knowledge lives in too many places with no routing between them. The wiki, the shared drive, the ticketing system, the chat archive, and three people's heads all hold part of the answer.

The second cause is that nobody owns findability. Teams own their content. Nobody owns the path from a question to the right piece of content across teams.

## Diagnostic questions

1. Pick five common questions from your support queue or chat channels. How long does it take a new employee to find each answer without asking anyone?
2. How many systems would someone need to search to answer a typical operational question?
3. What share of questions in your main help channels already have a documented answer?
4. How many of your top 20 documents require an access request for the people who need them?
5. When someone finds an answer, do they share the link or retype the answer?

## What moves it

- Consolidate or federate search so there's one front door, even if content lives in several systems.
- Route repeat questions to the documented answer, and track how often the same question comes back.
- Put the answer where the work happens: in the IDE, the ticket, the runbook, the CRM.
- Remove access friction on knowledge that's meant to be shared.

Buying a new search tool rarely fixes speed on its own. If the content underneath is scattered and ownerless, better search finds the wrong answer faster.
