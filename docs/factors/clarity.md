# Clarity

## Definition

Clarity is whether the person who finds the knowledge can understand it and act on it without a follow-up conversation. Clear knowledge fits its reader. It states the answer first, defines its terms, and makes the next step obvious.

Clarity beats complexity. A short page that answers the question outperforms a complete page that buries it.

## What zero looks like

- Documents that are accurate but only make sense to the team that wrote them.
- Readers who find the right page and still book a meeting with the author.
- Executive summaries that summarize the process instead of the decision.
- Acronyms and internal code names with no definitions.
- Runbooks that describe the system instead of telling you what to do.

## Structural causes

Clarity collapses when content is written for the author instead of the reader. Engineers document what they built. Product teams document what they shipped. Neither one documents what the next person needs to do.

The other cause is missing audience design. A single page tries to serve the new hire, the expert, and the executive at once, and serves none of them.

## Diagnostic questions

1. Hand three of your most-used documents to someone outside the owning team. Can they complete the task without asking a question?
2. Does each key document state who it's for and what it helps them do?
3. How many of your top documents put the answer or action in the first screen?
4. Do you have a shared glossary, and do documents link to it?
5. When people follow up with authors after reading a document, what do they ask? That's your clarity gap.

## What moves it

- Write for a named reader and a named task. If you can't name either, the page probably shouldn't exist.
- Put the answer, decision, or action first.
- Split content by audience instead of stacking every audience on one page.
- Treat follow-up questions as defect reports against the document.
- Use templates for recurring document types so readers know where to look.

Clarity is the factor documentation teams already work on. It matters, but it's rarely the constraint in organizations with a functioning docs function. Check trust and reusability before you fund another rewrite.
